## What you get from prototyping and usability testing {#what-you-get}

Each round answers one question your team has to decide before engineers build. Testing sits inside our [Product Design](/services/product-design) work.

- **The prototype the question needs.** Rough clickable screens when the order of steps is in doubt, realistic content and data when people must read and compare, and working code when the question is how the browser behaves.

- **People who match your users.** Existing customers, trial users or others who fit a profile we agree with you before the first session.

- **Realistic tasks.** Real goals with realistic records, so people work the way they would on an ordinary day.

- **Moderated sessions your team can watch.** What people do counts for more than what they say they like.

- **A report after each round.** The tasks, who took part, what we saw, what to change and what still needs testing.

- **A revised design,** ready for the next round or for your engineers.

**Who does the work.** A senior product designer plans each round, runs the sessions and leads the work, with an account lead keeping the rounds on schedule. Design engineers build the prototype when the question needs code.

**What we need from you.** The question at stake and the result that would change your team's mind, help reaching participants or agreement on a profile, and realistic data for the prototype.

**Where our part stops.** You get the findings and a revised design, and the build stays with your engineers. When only a browser can answer the question, [Coded Prototyping](/services/design-engineering/coded-prototyping) takes it further, and [Interaction & UI Design](/services/product-design/interaction-ui-design) carries a tested direction into every screen.

## Moderated usability testing for SaaS, with realistic data {#testing}

A SaaS task depends on the person's role, their data and years of habit. Strangers clicking through tidy screens tell you little about it. We recruit for each role the flow touches and give people records that look like their own, because thousands of rows, missing values and very long names change what people notice.

We test each group until the same problems keep repeating, then stop, and plan another round when the first raises new questions. Where the question is about the browser itself, such as drag and drop, live updates or a small screen, the prototype is built in code.

Products with AI inside need one more kind of test, because the same request can get a different answer each time and some answers will be wrong. We put realistic variations in front of people, wrong answers included, so the recovery can be designed before launch.

Example: for [Gold Image Printing](/case-studies/gold-image-printing-web-to-print-erp-modernisation), we watched staff use the existing system before redesigning it, and interactive prototypes let stakeholders review the new ordering and production flows before implementation.

## Questions about usability testing services {#faq}

### How is usability testing for SaaS different from testing a website?

SaaS tasks run longer and depend on a person's role, data and habits, so the people taking part need to do that work. We give them realistic records and tasks from their own day. A website test can often use a wider group of people and shorter tasks.

### What should we prototype first?

The assumption that would cost most if it turns out wrong. That might be a core workflow, an unfamiliar interaction, dense data or a browser behaviour nobody has tried yet.

### How many users do you need for usability testing?

Tcules tests each group of users until the same problems keep repeating, then stops. We plan each round around the question and the groups that matter, and run another when the first raises new ones.

### Can you test a prototype we built with AI tools?

Yes. Tcules tests a prototype built with AI tools like any other, with people who match your users. If paying customers are next, a [Prototype Readiness Review](/audits/prototype-readiness-review) shows what needs rebuilding first.

### What happens to test recordings and participant details?

Recordings, notes and participant details stay in tools we agree with you, and we sign your NDA and data processing agreement.

### How much does usability testing cost?

The cost depends on the number of rounds, the user groups involved and how realistic the prototype needs to be. Tcules prices testing up front once that scope is agreed, and one round on one flow is a strong place to start. [How pricing works](/how-we-work#engagement-shapes)
