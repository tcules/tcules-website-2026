# Experienced designers and engineers, working closely with your product team

Some clients bring Tcules one difficult workflow. Others bring an ambitious new product, a platform that needs to evolve, or a roadmap that never stands still. We shape the engagement around the work and bring experienced product designers, design engineers and developers close to the team making it happen.

You see the thinking, challenge the direction and work with the people responsible for the result.

## Three ways to work together: our product design engagement models {#engagement-shapes}

Different products need different kinds of help. Each of these is a starting point, shaped around your work.

### Start with one meaningful challenge

*A focused engagement*

You know something needs attention: a workflow is failing, an interface is holding users back, or a prototype built with AI may not be ready for real customers. We investigate, redesign or test one bounded part of the product, and you leave with something your team can act on.

**How it works:** an agreed scope, deliverable and fixed price. A pilot project works this way, and it's optional. The engagement ends with the work, and once it's paid for, you keep what we make.

*Typical starting points:* a [UX or product audit](/audits), a [prototype readiness review](/audits/prototype-readiness-review), one workflow or a design pilot.

### Take a product initiative forward

*A defined project*

You have an outcome in mind: a first release, a product redesign, a design system or a major piece of modernisation. We bring the right blend of design and engineering skills, establish what the work must achieve and take responsibility for the agreed scope.

**How it works:** scope, roles, milestones and fees agreed before work starts. We deliver designs for your engineers, or carry agreed parts through development.

*Typical starting points:* an [MVP](/services/mvp-development), a major workflow redesign, a [design system](/services/design-systems) or a new product module.

### Keep design close as your product grows {#ongoing}

*An ongoing partnership*

Your product keeps changing, and you need design help that already knows its users, constraints and history. A senior designer leads the work and does it, in your team's rhythm. An account lead keeps everything running, and specialists join as the roadmap calls for them.

**How it works:** a fixed monthly fee for an agreed team. Two weeks' notice changes the team or ends the work, and there is no minimum term.

*Best when:* design is a continuing responsibility, with no single finish line.

**Start wherever the work is.** Begin with any of the three, and move between them when the work gives us a reason to.

Example: **Dealpath.** A focused audit came first, then a targeted redesign of its listing-to-pipeline flow, which Dealpath's team then implemented. [See the Dealpath project](/case-studies/enterprise-workflow-redesign-dealpath-real-estate)

## Your outsourced product design team, working alongside yours {#your-team}

You work with the people doing the work. A senior designer stays hands-on, and we bring in research, design-system or engineering expertise when it adds value. We work in your design files, project tools and reviews, so your team keeps its own process.

We involve the people who know the product best: product managers, engineers, domain specialists and, when the question calls for it, customers. They bring context; we bring a fresh perspective and design judgement. The best work needs both.

Our Ahmedabad studio gives our designers and engineers room to examine work together, challenge a direction and refine the details before it reaches you.

## You see the thinking as well as the design {#working-loop}

The work stays in view between the kickoff and the final presentation. You see early questions, alternative directions, prototypes and choices while they can still change.

Sometimes research changes the problem. Sometimes a rough interaction makes a better idea obvious. Sometimes your team knows a constraint we could only learn from you. We bring those discoveries into the work and let the plan move with them.

Example: **Doodle** asked for scheduling automation. One interview shifted the question from who is free to how dependable each time is, and meeting priority became part of the MVP design. [Read the Doodle case](/case-studies/doodle-case-study-automating-enterprise-meeting-scheduling)

Then comes the craft. We make a complex screen clear at a glance, get the interaction and language right, resolve the states that are easy to overlook and make the whole experience feel considered. We critique and refine until the important details belong together.

## From design files into working software {#design-to-release}

Some product choices need to be tried in code. Real data, responsive behaviour, accessibility and technical constraints reveal things a static mockup can't show. Our design engineers explore those questions while the design is still open to improvement.

Example: **Benchmark Gensuite.** Coded interface prototypes let its product and engineering teams examine shared patterns before wider implementation. [Read the Benchmark Gensuite case](/case-studies/benchmark-gensuite-enterprise-suite-modernisation)

Your engineers can build from our designs, we can work in your codebase, or our developers can take on a defined part of the build. We agree that responsibility at the outset. Product Design stays central whichever route you choose.

## The understanding grows with the relationship {#product-knowledge}

A product has reasons behind its apparent quirks: user habits, earlier choices, technical trade-offs and plans still taking shape. We learn those reasons and keep the important ones where your team can see them, so anyone who joins later starts with the reasons in hand.

That is why continuity matters. An ongoing relationship can move from a difficult first problem to new features, a stronger system or a larger change, and each step starts from what the last one taught us. **Primelis**, for example, has worked with Tcules on its products for three years.

Our designers also use AI to inspect products, organise existing material, explore alternatives and check well-defined tasks, with a person judging every result. Your team sees the gain in the work itself, with no extra process to run. [How we use AI in Product Design](/how-we-work/ai-native-product-design)

## Outsourcing UX design to Tcules: the practical details {#essentials}

### Getting started {#first-steps}

Tell us what you're working on. After a first conversation, we send a written proposal: the scope, who would do the work, how we'd collaborate and the price. A fixed-price pilot is a good first step, and it's optional.

### Working across time zones {#time-zones}

Our studio is in Ahmedabad, India, and most clients work in other countries. We agree the hours we share before each project starts, and work in the tools your team already uses. You have direct access to the practitioners doing the work.

### Fees and notice {#change}

Focused engagements and defined projects are priced on their agreed scope before work starts. Ongoing work is a fixed monthly fee, with two weeks' notice to change the team or end the work, and no minimum term. Your proposal states every figure.

### Who decides, and who owns the work {#shared-ownership}

You keep the product direction: we bring options, trade-offs and a clear recommendation, and you decide. Once the work is paid for, the designs and code we make for you are yours. The agreement sets out handover, intellectual property, NDAs and release sign-off before work begins.

If your organisation has rules about AI tools or client data, we agree how they apply. [How we use AI on client projects](/how-we-work/ai-transparency)

## Questions about outsourcing UX design {#faq}

### How do I outsource UX design for a SaaS product?

Send a few lines about the product and what needs to change, and a senior designer replies. After a first conversation, you get a written proposal naming the work, the people and the price. A small pilot project is a sensible first step, and it is optional. [Getting started](#first-steps)

### Is a UX design retainer or a fixed project better?

A defined project suits clear goals and a known end, such as a redesign or a first release. Month-to-month work suits a product that keeps changing, because the roadmap can shift without a new contract. Both are priced before work starts.

### How much does it cost to outsource UX design?

The cost depends on the scope and the team, and it is always agreed before work starts. A pilot has a fixed price agreed up front, a defined project is priced on its agreed scope, and ongoing work is a fixed monthly fee. Your proposal states the figure.

### How can we try a design studio before a long contract?

Start with a pilot project on one real problem, such as a redesigned flow or a UI concept. It has a fixed price agreed up front, it ends when the work is done, and once it is paid for, you keep what we make.

### How do you handle confidential information and NDAs?

Send your security or supplier questionnaire when you receive our proposal. We answer it, and agree confidentiality and data rules with you, before work starts. If you need an NDA before you share anything sensitive, say so when you get in touch.

### Does Tcules offer UX consulting services?

Yes. A senior designer can advise your own team: reviewing a flow, testing a direction with users or ranking what to fix through an [audit](/audits). Advice and hands-on design work can sit in the same engagement, priced the same way.

### Can an outsourced design team work with our in-house designers?

Yes. We work in your design files, your design system and your review rhythm, and your designers see our work as it happens. Specialists such as a researcher or a design-systems specialist join when the work calls for them.
