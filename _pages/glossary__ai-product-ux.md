# What is AI Product UX? Meaning, principles and examples

AI Product UX is the design of how people understand, direct, check and undo what an AI model does inside a product. It matters once the model's answer shapes a real record, choice or commitment.

The product must then show where the answer came from, who can overrule it and how a mistake gets put right. Polish on the screen cannot supply those.

The work covers the user's task and the model's role. It also covers sources, uncertainty, permissions, testing and recovery. That is wider than adding a chat box and narrower than the whole AI engineering stack.

[Agentic UX](/glossary/agentic-ux) applies when the AI plans or acts across steps. A [human-in-the-loop workflow](/glossary/human-in-the-loop) sets where a person can step in with real effect.

## AI Product UX examples: ConnectX, Omny AI and BuildTwin {#practice}

AI Product UX starts with the work a person is trying to finish, and the part AI is allowed to play in it. In [ConnectX](/case-studies/ai-native-building-energy-product-connectx), a question asked in plain words opens a working view of the building, with the right data and assets on screen.

In [Omny AI](/case-studies/omny-ai-suggestion-trust-redesign), Tcules kept AI suggestions separate from the seller's decision until the person accepted them. In [BuildTwin](/case-studies/ai-quality-control-expert-review-buildtwin), the review model kept engineering judgement at the centre of AI quality control.

The three interfaces differ, and the same questions come back each time. What may the model do? What can the person see to check it? Who can override it? How does the product recover when the output is wrong?

## AI Product UX principles: what makes an AI product easy to trust {#principles}

An AI recommendation needs a visible goal, the reasons behind it, alternatives and a person who owns the final decision. An AI action also needs permission, a visible state and a way back.

A handful of principles carry most of the weight. Show where an answer came from, so people can check it. Keep the AI's suggestion apart from the person's own work until they accept it. Let the right person overrule the model. Plan how the product recovers when the output is wrong.

Read about [building trust in AI interfaces](/insights/building-trust-and-transparency-in-ai-first-user-interfaces).

## AI UX vs traditional UX: designing for output that varies {#comparison}

Traditional UX designs for software that gives the same result for the same input. AI UX also designs for output that varies, can be wrong and may sound confident either way. Sources, uncertainty, permissions and recovery join the usual flows and screens. The design work also sits apart from building the AI feature itself.

| Focus | Question | What you would look at |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional UX | How does a person finish the task with software that behaves the same way each time? | Flows, screens, states, content and usability tests |
| AI capability | What can the model or service produce? | Model output, technical tests and how it connects to the product |
| AI Product UX | How should a person use, check, control or reject that output in real work? | Realistic scenarios, how each state looks, the sources shown, who has authority and how recovery works |

## Terms related to AI Product UX {#related-terms}

[Agentic UX](/glossary/agentic-ux): the design of AI that carries out several steps on its own, including which steps need approval and how a wrong change is reversed.

[Human in the loop](/glossary/human-in-the-loop): a person reviewing an AI's work who has the information, authority and time to stop the result before it takes effect.

[Coded prototype](/glossary/coded-prototype): working software built to test what a design file can't show, such as an AI answer that appears word by word.

## AI Product UX FAQs {#faq}

### What does AI UX mean?

AI UX is the common name for what this glossary calls AI Product UX: the design of how people understand, direct, check and undo what an AI model does inside a product. It covers the person's task, the model's role, the sources shown, who can overrule a result and how mistakes get put right.

### How is AI UX different from traditional UX?

Traditional UX assumes software behaves the same way each time. AI UX designs for output that varies and can be wrong, so it adds visible sources, signs of uncertainty, permissions and a way to recover. The familiar work on flows, screens and usability still applies.

### What makes an AI product easy to trust?

People trust an AI product when they can see where an answer came from, change or reject it, and recover when it is wrong. Fit matters too: the AI should help with the task in front of them, inside limits the product makes clear.

### What are examples of good AI product UX?

In [Omny AI](/case-studies/omny-ai-suggestion-trust-redesign), AI suggestions stay apart from committed content until a person chooses them. In [BuildTwin](/case-studies/ai-quality-control-expert-review-buildtwin), expert review keeps engineering judgement at the centre of AI quality control. Both make the AI's part visible and leave the final say with a person.

## What AI Product UX work covers around the model {#next}

The work covers what the model contributes, what people can inspect and how recovery works when the output is wrong.

The field is widening as AI moves from answering to acting. Products now need designs for agents that change records, answers that arrive in stages and models that change between releases, and each raises the same questions of trust and recovery.
