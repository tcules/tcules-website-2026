# Design system strategy for scope, ownership and what to build first

Our design system strategy sets out what every product shares and who approves changes, so each new request gets a quick yes or no.

*Selected work: [Auxentios](/case-studies/auxentios-cpq-product-mvp-design) · [Doodle](/case-studies/doodle-case-study-automating-enterprise-meeting-scheduling) · [Gold Image Printing](/case-studies/gold-image-printing-web-to-print-erp-modernisation)*

## What you get from a design system strategy {#what-you-get}

A plan your teams can build from, agreed with the people who will build it and the people who will live with it.

- **What you already have, listed.** What already exists in Figma and in code, and which duplicates to merge.

- **What the system covers.** Every pattern your teams use, sorted into shared, product-specific, technical and still open, filled in for your products.

- **A structure for the system.** How colours, type, spacing and components are organised, so the system grows without a reshuffle, and where a theme layer handles brand differences.

- **Owners and contribution rules.** Who proposes, who builds, who signs off and who releases, for each kind of change.

- **A design system roadmap.** The components your products need first, in the order teams will use them, with the measures agreed before the build.

- **A business case in your numbers.** Which screens teams rebuild today, how often and in how many products, and what the first release would let them stop doing.

- **Rules for AI coding tools.** What they may assemble from the system on their own, and what still needs a designer's sign-off.

**Who does the work.** A senior designer leads the strategy and runs the conversations with every team, a design engineer reads the components already in your code, and an account lead keeps it moving.

**What we need from you.** Product, design and frontend leads from each team that will use the system, someone who can make product decisions, and access to your Figma files and code. Strategy goes wrong when engineering hears about it last.

**Where our part stops.** You get a plan, and the build is a separate choice. [Design System Implementation](/services/design-systems/design-system-implementation) builds it, [Governance & Adoption](/services/design-systems/governance-adoption) runs the owners and rules day to day, and a [Design System Audit](/audits/design-system-audit) looks at a system you already have.

## Selected design system strategy work {#selected-work}

### Auxentios: six roles mapped before the first component

Six specialist roles, from sales to pricing to maintenance, contribute to each quote in Auxentios' industrial quoting product. We worked out what each role needed from a quote before drawing any component, then turned the needs that repeated into components, shared values and layout rules.

## Design system scope: what belongs in the system, and who approves a change {#scope}

We sort the patterns your teams use into four groups, argued out once with the people who own them.

| Group | What belongs there | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Shared | Patterns every product should handle the same way | Form error messages, status labels, keyboard navigation |
| Product-specific | Work that belongs to one product's workflow | A pricing screen only one team uses |
| Technical | Code choices that support the system without changing what users see | How components are packaged for your code |
| Still open | Patterns that look shared but need more real use first | A filter two teams have built in different ways |

The last group matters most. Standardise a pattern too early and every team inherits the wrong answer at once. Our article covers [matching a design system to the size of your product](/insights/design-systems-for-saas-boosting-efficiency-and-ux-consistency).

Approval follows the reach of a change. A shared colour touches every product; one product's layout touches one team.

| Kind of change | Who approves it | How teams hear about it |
|---|---|---|
| A foundation value, such as a colour or a spacing step | The foundation owners in design and engineering | Release notes to every product, with time to move across |
| A new shared component | The system owners, after a product team brings the need | Added to the documentation and the next release |
| A product exception | That product's design lead, recorded with an owner | Listed where other teams can see it |
| A change that could break a product | The system owners, with each affected team | Announced ahead, with a migration guide and an end date for the old version |

## Questions about design system strategy {#faq}

### Do we need a design system at all?

A design system pays off when several teams or products keep solving the same interface problems. For one small team with one product, Tcules sizes the answer to match: often a shared component library and a few rules, built so they can grow into a full system.

### Can one design system serve several products or brands?

Yes, when the products share the same meaning underneath. In a multi-brand design system, differences that are only colour and type fit a theme layer; differences in how a product behaves need their own components. Products that behave very differently may need a shared core with separate layers on top.

### Who needs to be involved in a design system strategy?

The product, design and frontend leads of every team that will use the system, and one person who can make product decisions. Bring engineering in from the first conversation, because a plan they hear about last rarely survives the build.

### How do we build the business case for a design system?

Start with the work teams repeat today: the same component rebuilt in several products, and the time spent arguing which version is right. Tcules counts that from your own Figma files and code, then shows what the first release would remove.

### How do you measure design system ROI?

Agree the measures before the build. Useful ones are how many screens use shared components, how many local copies remain and how long a new screen takes from shared parts. Tcules sets the starting figures with you so later change shows up.

### Do we need a design system strategy consultant?

Bring in outside help when teams disagree, or when nobody has the time to talk to every team. Tcules runs the strategy with your leads, so the plan carries their authority as well as an outside view on scope.
