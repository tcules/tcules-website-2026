# Replatforming product design that keeps customers' workflows and saved work intact

During a replatforming, we decide with you what carries over and what customers see while both platforms run, so nobody loses saved work.

## What you get from replatforming product design {#what-you-get}

Your engineers move the data. We design what it means to customers when it arrives, as part of our [Product Modernisation](/services/product-modernisation) work.

- **What happens to each feature.** Moved as it is, fixed on the way, moved later or left behind, decided feature by feature with a reason.

- **The changeover months, designed.** Who sees which version, where their records live and how support tells the versions apart.

- **A plan for saved work.** What happens to customers' saved views, filters, reports, roles and drafts, agreed item by item with your engineers.

- **Names that still make sense.** Renamed features and moved settings carried over or explained, so help articles, training and habits survive the move.

- **Risky interactions tried in code** on the new platform, before the wider rollout.

- **Help with the platform choice,** if you start early enough: the workflows and saved work customers rely on, mapped so each candidate platform is judged with them in view.

**Who does the work.** A senior designer runs the customer side of the move and does the design, and our engineers build the risky interactions on the new platform. An account lead keeps the design in step with your migration plan.

**What we need from you.** The workflows and saved work customers rely on, the chosen or candidate platforms, and time with the engineers running the migration.

**Where our part stops.** How the data moves stays with your engineers, or with our [Software Development](/services/software-development) team for the parts you hand us. We define what each item should mean to the customer when it arrives.

## Platform migration UX: what carries over, and the months in between {#transition}

Every feature needs its own answer when you move, and the move is a chance to leave old problems behind.

| For each feature | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Move it as it is** | Customers rely on it and it still works well |
| **Fix it on the way** | The new platform finally makes a long-wanted change practical |
| **Move it later** | Moving it now would add risk to the migration |
| **Leave it behind** | Too few people use it to justify the cost of moving it |

For weeks or months, some customers use the new version while others stay on the old one. A migration without losing users depends on that period, so we design it: which terms and permissions apply where, and what support sees when a customer calls.

The things customers created need the most care. Some move automatically, some need translating and some need the customer to choose.

| What customers created | Where the move can go wrong | What we design |
|---|---|---|
| Saved views and filters | Fields carry new names on the new platform | A translation for each view, and a clear note where one could only move in part |
| Reports | Figures shift when the data is structured differently | A side-by-side check customers can trust before the old report goes |
| Roles and permissions | Role names and limits seldom match one to one | A mapping your admins approve before anyone moves |
| Drafts and work in progress | Half-finished items may not fit the new structure | A rule for each item type: finish it, move it or let the customer choose |

## Replatforming product design FAQs {#faq}

### What is replatforming?

Replatforming moves a product to new technology, such as a newer framework, a cloud service or a commerce platform, while keeping what it does for customers. Most moves also fix a few long-standing problems on the way, and old and new versions often run side by side for a while.

### What's the difference between replatforming, refactoring, redesign and a rebuild?

Refactoring restructures the code on the same platform, and customers notice little. A redesign changes the experience on the same technology. Replatforming moves the product to new technology, so old and new coexist for a while, and a rebuild replaces the structure itself.

### Can you help before we've chosen the new platform?

Yes. Tcules maps the workflows and customer data the new platform must support, so the choice can be judged with customers in view. If you're still deciding whether to move at all, start with a [Product Modernisation Assessment](/audits/product-modernisation-assessment).

### Do you handle website and ecommerce replatforming?

Yes, where the move changes how customers find things or buy. We redesigned GT Tools' Shopify store and developed it within the platform's limits, and we apply the same feature-by-feature method when a store moves platform.

### Who owns the technical migration?

Tcules owns the customer side: what people see, keep and have to learn. The technical migration sits with your platform or engineering team, or with our developers for the parts you hand us.

### We're already mid-migration. Is it too late?

Mid-migration is a good moment to start, because the in-between period is when customers feel the move most. Tcules starts from where the programme is and designs that period alongside the people moving the data.
