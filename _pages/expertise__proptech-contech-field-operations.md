## PropTech and related product design work {#selected-work}

### Dealpath: find the existing deal before adding a new one

Dealpath's acquisitions teams judge listings and track deals. Our audit found that Add to pipeline assumed every listing was a new deal, so the redesign searches for an existing deal first. Viewing, marking a comparable and passing stayed largely as users knew them, and implementation sat with Dealpath's team. [Read the Dealpath case](/case-studies/enterprise-workflow-redesign-dealpath-real-estate)

### BuildTwin: the engineer can overrule the AI

A drawing in BuildTwin's structural-engineering software passes from detailer to quality engineer to approver, with new AI checks in the middle. We turned the result list into a review workspace where each check links to its source, and an overrule needs a reason that stays on record. [Read the BuildTwin case](/case-studies/ai-quality-control-expert-review-buildtwin)

### Benchmark Gensuite: find your site from the car park

People using Benchmark Gensuite's safety and sustainability suite picked their site from the organisation's hierarchy, which suits a desk better than a site visit. Our redesign added Nearby, which lists sites by the user's location, as a coded prototype that Benchmark's engineers reviewed. [Read the Benchmark Gensuite case](/case-studies/benchmark-gensuite-enterprise-suite-modernisation)

## Construction software design for office, site and sign-off {#hard-parts}

Property and site software serves people who rarely sit together: an acquisitions team at desks, an engineer reviewing drawings, a trade on site with gloves on. The difficulty is keeping all of them working from one record of each place.

### One property, one identity on every screen

A property, project or site keeps the same name, status and key figures wherever it appears, from a full dashboard to a single map pin. When the dashboard, the map and the list each show it their own way, people second-guess what they are looking at.

Example: on Dealpath's listing card we kept all the property data and changed the reading order into three zones: image, source and status; identity and highlights; the core values for comparison. In the redesign, the card shrinks to fit the dashboard, a row beside the map and a pin's details, keeping its order in each.

### Drawings and photos kept with each sign-off

Drawings, documents, photos and notes stay attached to the finding they support, so nobody chases the source before acting. Whoever prepares, reviews and approves gets the actions and detail their step needs. When someone signs off work on a building, the product shows what they checked and why.

AI checks need the same record. The person who signs sees what the AI checked, what it could not see and what they are putting their name to. They can overrule it with a reason that stays on file.

In the UK, the Building Safety Act requires a "golden thread" of accurate building information for higher-risk buildings, kept up to date through design, construction and occupation. We design records where every change, check and approval stays tied to the drawing or document behind it.

### Designed for the site as well as the office

On site, signal drops, hands are full and interruptions never stop. Large tap targets, photos as the main record, offline saving and a clear "saved" state matter more than any dashboard.

Work saves on the device, the app shows what has and has not synced, and conflicts are explained in plain words when the signal returns.

People find a place by where they stand, and pick a task back up after an interruption. If you are weighing a separate app for site teams, our guide asks [whether your B2B SaaS product needs a mobile app](/insights/guides/saas-does-your-app-need-a-mobile-version).

In a new-home defects app, the screens follow from four answers agreed first with you and an experienced site manager. Who can close a defect? What does the buyer see, and when? How does a trade find the right plot with no signal? Which photo counts as the record?

### Tenants, managers and contractors on one job

Property managers juggle tenants, contractors and owners around one building. Repairs, leases, inspections and payments each have their own status, and tenants want to know when someone will come. We design portals where each party sees its part of the same job and who acts next.

## Questions about PropTech product design {#faq}

### Do you design tools for people working on site?

Yes. Tcules designs for people on site, who need to find a location by where they stand, record what they see and pick up a task after an interruption. Benchmark Gensuite's Nearby view, above, is one example.

### Can you improve one workflow inside an established property platform?

Yes. At Dealpath, Tcules redesigned one listing workflow to fit the existing product and its technical limits, then handed it to Dealpath's engineers ready to build. That work grew out of an audit of the product.

### How do you design AI inspection and review tools?

Every AI result shows what it is based on, missing inputs are flagged, and a qualified person can overrule the result with a reason that stays on record. That is the review we designed for BuildTwin.

### Have you designed real estate investment software?

Yes. For Dealpath, deal-management software used by real estate acquisition teams, Tcules audited the product and redesigned the listing-to-deal workflow. The listing card leads with what helps a team judge an opportunity, and the new flow searches existing deals before a new one is created.
