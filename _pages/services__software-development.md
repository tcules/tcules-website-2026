## Selected SaaS development work {#selected-work}

### ConnectX: proved in a demo, then taken to production

ConnectX wanted people to ask about a building's energy in plain words and get a working view back. We proved the product first in a coded demo on sample data, so ConnectX could judge it whole. Then we took it to production ourselves, and the module has been live since late 2025.

### GT Tools: a Shopify store that answers buyers beside the price

GT Tools sells specialist automotive-glass equipment on Shopify. We designed the store around what buyers check before they buy, such as compatibility, stock and warranty. Then we developed it ourselves, with custom work where Shopify needed it, keeping the price and buy button in view throughout.

[Read the GT Tools case](/case-studies/gt-tools-shopify-product-discovery)

### The Wandering Raven: a designed learning world, carried onto Moodle

The Wandering Raven teaches creative writing to learners aged eight to eighteen, partly on Moodle. After the research and design, we installed and configured the Moodle plugins and made a custom theme. Design and platform stayed with one team, and the client could concentrate on its courses.

## What you get from our SaaS development services {#what-you-get}

We build a first release, a large new feature or the next stage of a live product, with designers in the build team.

- **Working software:** screens, the systems behind them and integrations, built in small releases.

- **Every workflow designed before it is coded,** including its loading, empty and error screens, so engineers build an agreed answer.

- **Tests and release checks:** agreed before the build, so "ready" means the same thing to both teams.

- **Code, tests and notes in your repository,** written for the engineers who come after us.

- **Support through each release,** then a clean handover, or we stay on and keep building.

**Who does the work.** A senior designer leads each build, and one of our senior engineers reviews every change before it joins your codebase. Developers and designers work as one team, with an account lead as your day-to-day contact.

**What we need from you.** Whatever exists, from research and specifications to a prototype or a live codebase. We also need access to your repository, environments and release process, and someone who signs off scope and releases.

**Where our part stops.** The scope and each release are yours to sign off. Large cloud moves, and the hosting your platform team runs, stay with that team. We answer for the quality of what we build.

## What we agree before the build starts, and who answers for what {#scope}

| | What we agree |
|---|---|
| **What it should do** | Users, roles, tasks, rules and edge cases |
| **What it touches** | Screens, the systems behind them, data and integrations |
| **When it's ready** | The testing and checks it must pass before release |
| **Who owns what** | Code, reviews, security, support and agreed hosting, each with a named owner |

Our engineers use AI coding tools for well-defined work, so more of the time goes on what customers notice, from the first-run screen to the error message on a bad day. People review every change and make the decisions on architecture, data and security.

*Illustration, not client work*

A field-service SaaS company wants customers to book their own visits inside the scheduling product it already runs. We would build the booking screens, the booking API with its availability rules, and the connection to the scheduling service, while the company keeps the scheduling engine, accounts, billing and platform.

The build is ready when a customer can book, move and cancel a visit and two customers can't take the same slot. The office sees each booking at once, with a record of who changed what.

It ships behind a switch to a few customers first, and the code, tests and run notes go into the company's repository.

## Questions about our SaaS development services {#faq}

### Can you build our whole first release?

Yes. We take a complete first release from design to launch, covering the frontend, backend, integrations and testing. That work runs through [Design-led MVP Development](/services/mvp-development), where the release is scoped around what early customers need first.

### Can you work in our existing codebase?

Yes. We study how your code handles data, permissions and components before changing it, and follow your conventions, code review and release process. Changes reach your codebase through the same reviews your own engineers use.

### What is your SaaS development process?

We plan from the research, specifications and code you already have, and design each workflow, error screens included, before coding starts. Then we build in small releases with designers alongside, test each release against what was agreed, and hand over the code, tests and notes or keep building.

### Do we need a discovery phase before SaaS development?

For whatever is still unclear, yes. When the product idea itself is untested, a short research and design stage comes first, and we say so before pricing the build.

### What does SaaS development cost?

The cost follows what the product must do and connect to, so we price each build up front once the scope is agreed. A pilot of one feature has a fixed price, and [How We Work](/how-we-work#engagement-shapes) explains how defined projects and ongoing work are priced.

### Is Tcules a design studio or a SaaS development company?

Tcules is a product design studio that also builds software, so ours is design-led software development. Product design is the centre of the work, and design and engineering sit in one team from the first screen to release.

### Is Tcules a SaaS development company in India?

Our studio is in Ahmedabad, India, and we have worked with clients in 19 countries since 2017. [How We Work](/how-we-work#time-zones) explains our working hours and how we run the collaboration day to day.
