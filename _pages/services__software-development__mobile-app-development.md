## What you get from mobile app development {#scope}

An iOS and Android app for the few tasks that belong on a phone, built to work where your users actually are.

- **The mobile tasks chosen.** We list the jobs people do away from a desk and pick the few that belong on a phone, instead of every web screen shrunk down.

- **Offline behaviour.** What people can do without signal, what the phone stores, what happens when someone else changed the same record, and a clear sign of what has saved.

- **Notifications.** Which ones are worth the interruption, and which screen each opens, even when it is tapped hours later.

- **Device features.** Camera, location, files and signing on screen, and what happens when someone taps "Don't allow".

- **Every state, tried on a phone.** Offline, syncing and a refused permission each get a screen, and prototypes run on real devices so people test tasks where they would do them.

- **Both platforms built.** One cross-platform codebase in most cases, or native apps when the device does the heavy lifting.

- **Store release.** Listings, submissions, crash reports and updates that keep older versions working.

- **The same records and rules as your web app.** The app shares records, rules and words with your web product, usually through the same backend and API, so customers recognise it between phone and desk.

**Who does the work.** Designers and developers work it out together, because a syncing rule or a notification changes the screens and the code at once. An account lead keeps it running.

**What we need from you.** The jobs people do away from a desk, the phones they carry and how good the signal is. The web product's records and rules, and someone who can choose which tasks go mobile.

**Where our part stops.** You can stop at design: your mobile engineers get designs for every state, phone prototypes and notes on offline use, notifications and permissions. After launch, your team takes over releases, or we look after the app through [Product Maintenance & Evolution](/services/software-development/product-maintenance).

## When a mobile companion app is worth building {#format}

An app is worth building when the task needs a phone. One of these is usually true:

**The work happens away from a desk.** Carers between visits, engineers on site, a sales team in the car park.

**Signal comes and goes.** People need to keep working and to trust that what they entered has saved. The rule for syncing after a dead zone shapes what a field engineer sees as much as any layout.

**The phone does part of the job.** Taking photos, scanning, finding a location or signing on screen.

**Timing matters.** A notification has to reach the right person, and tapping it has to open the right screen.

**People open it many times a day.** Short, repeated tasks are quicker from a home screen than a browser tab.

Still weighing it up? Our guide, [Does my SaaS need a mobile app? A moment-by-moment test](/insights/guides/saas-does-your-app-need-a-mobile-version), walks through the questions.

## React Native or native iOS and Android: which suits your app? {#platforms}

| Option | What it means | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **One cross-platform app** | A single codebase, in a framework such as React Native, released to both stores | Most SaaS companion apps, where features should reach iPhone and Android together |
| **Native iOS and Android** | Separate apps built with each platform's own tools | Apps that lean hard on the camera, sensors, background work or offline maps |
| **A responsive web app** | Your web product made to work well on a phone's browser | Occasional mobile use with steady signal and no device features |
| **Design only, your team builds** | Designs for every state, phone prototypes and written notes | Companies with mobile engineers and no product designers |

We talk the trade-off through with you before anything is built.

## Questions about mobile app development for SaaS {#faq}

### How does a field app work without signal?

We agree with you which tasks must keep working offline, what the phone stores and what happens when someone else changed the same record in the meantime. The app then shows clearly what has saved and what is still waiting to send.

### Do you handle App Store and Google Play releases?

Yes. We prepare store listings and submissions, set up crash reporting and plan updates so customers on older versions keep working. Your team can take over releases at any point.

### Can you design the app and let our team build it?

Yes. We designed OctiFi's lending app for OctiFi's own engineers to build. Your engineers get designs for every state, prototypes they can try on a phone, and written notes on offline use, notifications and permissions.

### Does a SaaS mobile app need its own backend?

Usually it shares the backend and API of your web product, with a few additions for syncing, clashing edits and push notifications. Keeping one backend means the app and the web product follow the same rules. [Backend & API Development](/services/software-development/backend-api-development) covers that side of the work.
