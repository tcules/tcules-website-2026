# Redesign or rebuild worksheet: compare every route before you commit

The free Audit, Redesign, Modernise or Rebuild worksheet compares the routes for one part of your product, including how you would get from the old version to the new one.

A rebuild proposal can describe the destination in detail and leave the way there almost blank. Customers still need their records, integrations must keep running and someone has to support both versions until the move is done.

You leave with a brief that sets out the case for and against each route, what you still need to find out and the next test before you commit.

[Use the worksheet](#workspace)

## How to use the rebuild decision worksheet in four steps {#how-to-use-it}

1. **Pick one part with clear edges.** Quoting, approving an order or running a saved report: something you can judge on its own. The next section shows how to choose.

2. **Line up at least two routes.** Include keeping what you have where that is realistic, and fill in a row of the comparison table for each.

3. **Count the cost of the move.** Add migration, running old and new together, support and switching the old version off, with your assumptions beside each estimate.

4. **Mark each route and name the next test.** Record where each route stands, then take the brief to the people who will deliver the change.

## Start with one part of the product, such as quoting or reporting {#pick-one-part}

Choose a piece of work such as creating a quote, approving an order or running a saved report. Note who depends on it, what is getting expensive and why change is on the table now. Bring a product owner, an engineer and someone who knows how it is used day to day.

Several parts may share the same data or services. Compare them one at a time to see what each needs, and note the technical ties between them as you go.

## Rebuild vs refactor in practice: a report builder with saved reports {#example}

*Illustration, not client work.* A report builder has become expensive to extend. Rebuilding its editor looks attractive, but saved reports depend on the old query service.

Compare three routes: a focused repair, a redesign of the editor that keeps the existing service, and replacing the query service in stages.

Before choosing the third, test whether a sample of real saved reports still return the right results, and whether old and new can run side by side. If they can't, the order of work has to change.

Testing real saved reports tells the routes apart. A rating such as "technical cost: high" leaves them looking the same.

## Modernisation options compared: seven routes side by side {#workspace}

Compare at least two routes that could work, including keeping what you have where that is realistic. For each, record the problem it solves, what you know and the work of moving. Add what it depends on, what would count as done and the finding that would rule it out.

| Route | When it's worth a look | What the comparison must include |
|---|---|---|
| **Audit** | Before or during the choice, while the facts are still thin | The questions the audit has to answer before a route is chosen |
| **Redesign** | The task, the information or the interaction is what holds people back | What existing users must relearn, rules that change, and the engineering the redesign needs |
| **Modernise: keep and repair** | Current behaviour still works, and one contained issue is causing the pressure | Ongoing support cost, vendor support and the cost of waiting |
| **Modernise: move to a new platform** | Hosting or platform limits are driving the move | Compatibility, what the data means, how integrations behave and running costs |
| **Modernise: replace in stages** | Some parts can change while the useful behaviour stays | Untangling dependencies, old and new running together, migration and temporary work |
| **Rebuild or replace** | The current code, or the product's fit, can't justify building on it any further | The business rules to recover, gaps in fit, the full cost of the move and how you would exit |
| **Retire** | It no longer earns its keep | Who depends on it, contracts, records to keep and a substitute where needed |

An audit comes first when the facts are thin, because it shows which of the other routes fits. Modernising covers three kinds of change, so it takes three rows. Retiring a part sits beside rebuilding: both end the old version.

## Cost of rebuilding software: count every step of the move {#cost}

Include discovery, building and data migration. Add changes to integrations, running old and new side by side, support, training and finally switching the old version off.

Note the estimate range, the period it covers, your assumptions and where each figure came from. Mark "unknown" where nobody has looked yet, and keep zero for costs you know are nil.

Keep expected benefits in their own column, each with a note of how you would measure it, and leave guessed revenue out of the savings.

## Mark where each route stands before anyone commits {#reading-your-results}

Mark each route "candidate", "needs evidence", "ruled out" or "preferred for next test". A preference stays provisional until the evidence is written down and someone owns the decision.

The worksheet draws on [AWS's guidance on assessing an application portfolio](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/application-portfolio-assessment-guide/introduction.html). It turns your team's answers into reasoning on paper and a list of what to check next. That is exactly what the people who look inside the system will ask for when they assess architecture, hidden dependencies and cost.

## When to rebuild software, and who this worksheet is for {#who-its-for}

It is for the people who own an established product and must choose a route. That usually means a head of product, a CTO or an engineering lead, with a product owner beside them.

The usual trigger is one part of the product that has become expensive to change, a platform losing vendor support, or a rebuild proposal already on the table.

A newer trigger is joining the list: AI features and agents that need clean records, clear permissions and actions they can call. A part that can't offer those is a candidate for change even while customers are happy with it.

The worksheet puts the case for a rebuild on paper beside the smaller routes.

## Audit before a rebuild when the routes are still disputed {#next}

Review the finished worksheet with the people who will deliver and run the change. ## Redesign or rebuild FAQs {#faq}

### Should we redesign or rebuild our software?

Look at where the problem sits. If the task, the information or the interaction holds people back, a [product redesign](/services/product-modernisation/product-redesign) reaches it with less disruption. If the code or the product's fit is the limit, a rebuild may be the honest answer, provided you can pay for the move as well as the build.

### When is a full rewrite worth it?

A rewrite is worth it when building on the current code costs more than replacing it, and you can recover the business rules it holds. Before you choose it, compare a staged replacement and a contained refactor. Either may keep the useful behaviour while the troublesome parts change.

### How do we compare audit, redesign, modernise and rebuild?

Give each realistic route its own row in the table above. For every route, record the problem it solves, what you know, the work of moving, what it depends on and the finding that would rule it out. An audit comes first when the facts are still thin.

### What does rebuilding software really cost?

More than the build itself. Add discovery, data migration, integration changes, running old and new side by side, support, training and switching the old version off. The worksheet records each estimate as a range, with its period, assumptions and source, and marks "unknown" where nobody has looked yet.

### Should we audit before a rebuild?

Audit first when the facts are thin or the team disagrees on the route, because an audit shows which route fits before money goes into one. Our [Product Modernisation Assessment](/audits/product-modernisation-assessment) looks inside that part of your product and sets out an order for the change.
