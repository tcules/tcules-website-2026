# Best SaaS design agencies in 2026: eight studios compared by fit

By [Rakesh Chitroda](/insights/authors/rakesh-chitroda) · Published 23 October 2026 · Updated 26 October 2026 · 11 min read

A shortlist of SaaS design agencies is easy to find and hard to use, because many lists are written by an agency that puts itself first. This one compares eight studios, Tcules among them, from what each says on its own website. They appear in alphabetical order, and each gets a plain line on the buyer it suits best.

We read every studio's site on 23 October 2026. Studios change their terms, so check the details with any studio before you sign.

**Key takeaways**

- Seven studios that SaaS buyers meet in Google results and AI answers, plus Tcules, compared from each studio's own website as read on 23 October 2026.
- They differ most in three ways: whether they also build, how ongoing work is structured, and how much brand and website work they take on.
- For an embedded designer, Eleken (on a monthly subscription) and UX Studio (working inside your own tools) set out their models most plainly. For brand, website and product together, Clay covers the widest scope.
- For complex multi-role workflow software that should be carried into code, Cieden, Neuron and Tcules each lead with that kind of work.
- Test any shortlist with a small first project before signing up for ongoing work.

## What is a SaaS design agency? {#what-is-a-saas-design-agency}

A SaaS design agency designs the software product itself: the screens, the workflows behind them, the roles and permissions, and the states a record moves through. Most also run research and usability testing, and many build design systems. Some go further and write production code.

A marketing website for a SaaS company is a different job, usually handled by web design agencies. Searches for "SaaS web design agency" mostly lead there. Everything in this guide is about the product your customers log in to.

## How we compared the SaaS design agencies {#how-we-compared}

- **What each studio leads with.** The kind of product and buyer each studio's own site puts first.
- **What it covers beyond design.** Research, design systems, front-end code, full development, brand and websites.
- **How work starts.** A free trial, a paid first project or a proposal after discovery, as each site describes it.
- **How ongoing work is structured.** Subscription, retainer, time and materials or fixed scope, with any minimum term the site states.
- **Where the team is.** The city or region each site names, because it shapes working hours.
- **What we left out.** Prices, review scores and client results. They change often, and a studio's own figures are hard to compare. Check review platforms such as Clutch for current ratings.

We did not score or rank the studios. A ranking would mean little when we are one of the eight.

## SaaS design agencies compared one by one {#the-eight-studios}

### Cieden

Cieden describes itself as remote-first, with teams across Europe and North America, and puts complex B2B platforms first: enterprise systems such as ERPs and CRMs, AI-native products and customer-facing B2B SaaS. Beyond design it offers product and project management, dedicated design teams and web app development. It offers a no-risk start, with early concept work you pay for only if you like it. Work then runs on time and materials or on a fixed-estimate partnership, with a monthly rate for extended engagements.

**Best for:** complex B2B platforms that want a dedicated team, with product management alongside the designers.
**Worth knowing:** Cieden also publishes its own comparison of SaaS design agencies, so read its list with that in mind, as you should read this one.

### Clay

Clay is a branding and UX agency headquartered in San Francisco, with offices across the US and in Europe. Its site covers brand identity, websites, product design, content and development, and its client list runs from startups to large enterprises. It names three engagement models, time and materials, fixed fee and retainer, and recommends one after discovery.

**Best for:** companies that want brand, marketing website and product designed by one agency.
**Worth knowing:** product design is one of several practices here, alongside brand, web and content.

### Eleken

Eleken presents itself as a UI/UX design agency for B2B SaaS, with an office in Kyiv and a US entity in Delaware. It covers redesigns, MVP design, UX audits, design systems and team extension, and its design engineering work delivers a working front end for the client's developers to connect. Designers join on a monthly subscription, full or part time, with a two-month minimum contract, and a free three-day trial comes first.

**Best for:** SaaS teams that want an embedded designer on a simple subscription.
**Worth knowing:** its development work stops at the front end, which your own engineers connect to the back end.

### MetaLab

MetaLab describes itself as a global team with Canadian roots and says it makes interfaces. Its site names Slack and Google among the companies it works with, says it has worked since 2006, and says it helps clients design, build and ship products. It does not describe its engagement models, so ask how the team would be set up for your product.

**Best for:** products that want an interface design studio with a long track record.
**Worth knowing:** expect to learn the engagement terms in conversation, as the site gives few details.

### Neuron

Neuron is a San Francisco UX/UI agency focused on enterprise software, with examples in analytics, CRM, finance and HR platforms, and work on AI products. It lists product strategy, UX/UI design and DesignOps as core services, with design systems, accessibility and front-end engineering alongside. Its site does not describe engagement models.

**Best for:** enterprise software teams, especially those that also want help running their own design operation.
**Worth knowing:** ask how the team would be structured, as the site does not say.

### Phenomenon Studio

Phenomenon Studio lists offices in Tallinn, Lugano and Dover in the US, and calls itself a design and engineering partner for scaling and regulated companies in healthtech, edtech, SaaS and fintech. It offers design, full development, MVP builds, redesigns, branding and websites. Teams range from one embedded designer to a full product squad.

**Best for:** regulated products that need design and development from one partner.
**Worth knowing:** its scope runs from brand and websites to full development, so be clear which part you are buying.

### Tcules

Tcules is a digital product design studio in Ahmedabad, India, working on new and established software with a particular focus on SaaS. Product design leads, and design engineering and software development sit in the same practice, so the work can stop at designs for your engineers, a finished interface or a defined part of the application. A senior designer leads and does the work, with an account lead alongside. A small pilot at a fixed price agreed up front is a good first step, and it is optional. Ongoing work runs at a fixed monthly fee, with no minimum term and two weeks' notice to change the team or end.

**Best for:** SaaS teams with multi-role workflow software that want senior designers to shape the product and carry it into code, starting small.
**Worth knowing:** the team is in India and overlaps four to five hours with US working days, extending that when the work needs it. Brand identity is not among its services.

### UX Studio

UX Studio is based in Budapest and positions itself as a UX partner for B2B SaaS teams, with research at the centre. It offers UX/UI design, research, consultancy and development, and its FAQ adds design systems and UX audits. It offers a free five-day trial that leaves you with two or three designed screens. Engagements are proposed with a fixed price or rate card, and its designers join your Slack, Linear and Figma.

**Best for:** research-led SaaS teams that want designers working inside their own tools.
**Worth knowing:** the model is built around an embedded team working in weekly cycles.

## Which SaaS design agency fits your situation {#which-fits}

- **You need a first release designed and built.** Most of these studios list development alongside design: Cieden, Clay, MetaLab, Phenomenon Studio, Tcules and UX Studio, with Eleken and Neuron offering front-end work. Ask each how much of the build it would own, and who keeps the design intact in code. Our guide to [taking a vibe-coded app to production](/insights/vibe-coded-app-production-ready) covers what to check if you already have a prototype.
- **Your product is complex multi-role workflow software.** Cieden, Neuron and Tcules each lead with this kind of work. Ask each for a workflow they untangled, and who decided what.
- **You want one designer added to your team quickly.** Eleken and UX Studio describe the simplest embedded models, with trials before you commit.
- **You need brand, website and product together.** Clay covers the widest scope, and Phenomenon Studio also takes on brand and web.
- **You work in a regulated field.** Phenomenon Studio puts regulated products first. Whoever you choose, ask how they build your compliance requirements into the design as well as the code.

## SaaS design agency or development company: what is the difference? {#agency-or-development-company}

A development company builds to a specification. A SaaS design agency decides what the specification should be: which workflows the product supports, how each role sees them and what happens when something goes wrong. Some studios, Tcules among them, do both in one practice, which keeps design decisions intact on the way into code. If you already have a finished specification and need build capacity, a development company is usually the better fit. Our guide to [choosing a product design agency or studio](/insights/guides/how-to-choose-a-product-design-agency) sets out which kind of partner fills which gap.

## Best SaaS design agency FAQs {#faq}

### How much do SaaS design agencies charge?

Most charge by time and materials, a fixed fee for defined scope, or a monthly subscription or retainer for ongoing work. Few publish prices, so ask for a written proposal. Tcules prices pilots and defined projects up front and ongoing work at a fixed monthly fee, as set out on [How We Work](/how-we-work).

### Should we choose a SaaS design agency that also builds?

Choose one that builds if you have no engineering team, or if your engineers are stretched and the design is likely to change in code. If your own engineers will build, a studio that hands over clean designs and components is enough. Either way, ask who keeps the design intact once building starts.

### Is it a risk to hire a SaaS design agency in another country?

The real risks are working hours and handover. Agree shared hours and a named lead before work starts, and test the arrangement on a small first project. Most of the studios here work with clients in other countries as a matter of course.

### How do we test a SaaS design agency before committing?

Start with a small, real piece of work: one workflow, one redesigned flow or an audit. Judge the result and the way the team worked, then decide on anything larger. Several studios here offer a free trial or a paid pilot for exactly this.

### Which SaaS design agency suits an early-stage startup?

An early-stage startup usually needs a first release designed and built, or one designer added quickly. For the first, ask the studios that list development alongside design how much of the build each would own. For the second, Eleken and UX Studio describe the simplest embedded models, each with a free trial.
