Each kind of content has one shape, picked for its job ("first identify the content's job and then
pick an appropriate shape"):

- **Rows and board.** Items are split by a fine hairline, half the hairline width, not thick rules or
  tiles ("too many horizontal lines").
- **Turn (four short lines).** A 2 × 2 set of tiles, each headed by a glyph. The marked one has a
  peridot edge and a faint peridot wash, not a solid fill. The outside quote is cited with a rule at
  its start and pinned to the foot with its note.
- **Steps.** One vertical numbered list, never two columns.
- **Examples of another product's words.** Each sits full width on a drawn screen, named in its bar.
  The words are set in the sans at one size, with no glyph. They are not shown as screenshots of real
  pages ("horrible without context"), not in serif and not in a size that singles one out.
- **Plates over a list.** The plate is full width, with the drawing centred at the same height (Sketch
  `widen`).
- **Notes.** Set at body-ink contrast. A note that only labels the frame ("Worked example") is cut.

**Why.** Each review note on a shape (lines that read as floating text, a quote with no clear job,
crowded rules) traced back to a shape picked without asking what the content does.

**The mistake it prevents.** Unlabelled lines floating under a title, tiles on every list, serif
examples, and screenshots of other companies' pages cropped to a passage.

**How to apply.** In the `SocialFrame` template in `tcules-social`. Chapter 10 §B2 lists content rows
that predate these.
