A glyph drawn on a flat isometric surface (a tick on a button top, a mark on a tile) is projected
onto that plane with the glyph's down running down and to the left. On a plane spanned by
x → (0.9, 0.45) and y → (−0.9, 0.45), glyph (a, b) maps to screen
(0.9·(a − b), 0.45·(a + b)). Then a tick, a pencil and a cross read the right way round from the
viewer's side.

**Why.** Glyphs drawn upright on the screen, or projected with down along the other axis, read as a
plus, a mirrored L and a pen pointing down.

**The mistake it prevents.** Three rounds on the approve, edit and reject cover, including moving the
buttons onto another surface, which the owner called "overcomplicated".

**How to apply.** Every isometric illustration that puts a glyph on a surface, on the site or in
`tcules-social`.
