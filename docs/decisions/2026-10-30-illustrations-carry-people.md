"People become part of the illustration rules." An isometric illustration may draw people. That ends ch05
§4a's "Figures: none" and the matching line in this entry's predecessor. Everything else in that entry
stands as ch05 §4a–§4d states it:
- flat planes in 2:1 parallel projection;
- blue draws the system, with one solid blue plane;
- **peridot marks the one carried item.** The owner: "not lime. peridot, as it has been." The colour is
  peridot, never "lime", and it keeps the carried-item role. It is not a general focal highlight.
- no gradients, shadows or new hues.

How a person is drawn is Jatin's to design. It covers the Tcules person, and a designer and a developer who
each read as their own (call, 30 Oct). Until that lands, the pawn figure in the round-3 banners and
Bhumika's finals is the working form.

**Why.** The banners need "whose world". Round 3's blind tests read pictures with people as the buyer's
moment, and Jatin is already designing the Tcules person.

**The mistake it prevents.** Stripping the people out of Bhumika's finals to satisfy "Figures: none".
Also, renaming the accent "lime" and using it as a highlight on whatever matters, which
docs/research/illustration-evolution-2026-10-30.md § 2 row 5 had proposed.

**How to apply.** People may appear in isometric illustrations. A photograph still stands wherever people
are the subject: the team, the studio, About (ch05 §1, §3). Peridot follows ch05 §4b: one solid, plus its
echoes and path. At the next `/dbf-update`, ch05 §4a's Figures row changes and §1's "never depicts a
person" is narrowed to "never in place of a team photograph".
