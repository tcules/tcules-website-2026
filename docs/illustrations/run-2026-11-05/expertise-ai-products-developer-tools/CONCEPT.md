## The object

The hub has no object for this industry. Picked: **the developer's keyboard**, drawn the way the hub draws its
objects (a simple isometric volume, solid blue as the hero, white keys with brandeis-200 sides). It says
"developer tools" at a glance with no screen and no lettering; the AI half comes from the small "AI" badge on
the result.

## Candidate ideas

1. **Press Enter, get a result you can check.** On a developer's desk a blue keyboard, its one lime Enter key
   pressed by a developer; behind it on the same desk the first result lies flat, marked AI, with a line of code
   and the three checks the model ran, and a reviewer across the desk has a hand on its sign-off.
2. **The rubber duck.** A developer explains code to a rubber duck on a desk while an AI reply comes back. Clever,
   but a metaphor that needs the in-joke; fails the three-second read.
3. **The AI chip under review.** A large chip marked AI on a tray with a reviewer's tick stamp beside it. Stock, and
   it says "hardware", not product design.

**Pick: 1.** It is the page's own lead in one picture: "gets developers to a working first result" (the pressed
Enter) "and lets reviewers see what the model checked before they sign off" (the ticked card and its waiting
sign-off button, the reviewer beside it). It keeps the round-3 pick's "first call, clean response" idea, which
Jatin called "right, in a way", but trades the abstract blue code panel and the lime rod for a real object.

**Arrangement, checked against the siblings:** one white desk on four legs in the middle of the plate, the
keyboard at its front and the result laid flat behind it, one person at each side of the desk. Nothing upright:
no screen on a foot with a person at it (climatetech, proptech, martech), no laptop (commerce, data), no phone or
tablet, nothing rising out of the object (healthtech), no stairs (edtech), no tile (proptech), no folder (B2B). It
is the only sibling built on a piece of furniture at human height.

## The drawing

- **Idea:** "They get a developer from pressing Enter to a working first result, and show the reviewer what the
  model checked before they sign off."
- **Drawn:** the family's dotted floor, dashed edge and soft inset. On it stands a plain white desk (top 282 × 280
  floor units, 40 high, brandeis-200 edges, four thin legs). Across the desk's front lies the keyboard at the hub's
  scale: a low solid-blue wedge (blue top, brandeis-600 front, brandeis-700 end, 5 high at the front rising to 11
  at the back, a slim 7-unit margin round the keys) carrying five real rows of keycaps, each a white cap top on a
  brandeis-100 skirt outlined in brandeis-500: number row with a wide Backspace, Tab, wide Caps, two Shifts and a
  long space bar. At the right end of rows two and three is the one lime key, a tall Enter, pressed slightly down.
  The developer, a white client pawn marked with code brackets, stands at the desk's right end; a short light arm
  (blue-outlined) grows from the shoulder and the round hand rests on Enter's lime top. Behind the keyboard, laid
  flat on the desk, is the result: a white sheet with a brandeis-200 edge carrying an outlined sign-off pill with
  a tick, title bars and an "AI" badge in its top row, a pale code row (`< >` and two lines of output) and three
  ticked lines (what the model checked). The reviewer, a white pawn marked with a tick, stands behind the desk's
  back edge facing the viewer, the desk hiding the lower part of the body, with a short shoulder arm reaching

- **Composition:** the desk sits on the plate's centre; the press is at the front right, the sign-off at the back,

- **Accent:** one lime, the Enter key. The sign-off pill is outlined, not lime: it is the reviewer's to press.
- **Alt:** Isometric illustration for AI product and developer tool design. On a dotted floor stands a white desk.
  On it lies a low blue keyboard with white keys and one lime Enter key, pressed by a developer marked with code
  brackets who stands at the desk's end. Behind the keyboard, laid flat on the desk, is the first result: a white

## Rounds

1. Keyboard front-centre, card mid-plate: the developer stood in front of the keyboard covering its corner, the
   reviewer's arm was a long stick up to the card, and the box was 1.9:1 (too wide).
2. People moved (reviewer to the card's left with no arm, developer to the keyboard's right), viewBox trimmed;
   the developer's arm became a long pointer across the keys.
3. Enter moved to the front right, the developer computed to stand just right of it, so the arm is short; card
   moved to the back corner to raise the box.
4. Card shifted right and made taller (box 1.49:1, inside 5:4–3:2); an outlined sign-off button added at its foot;
   the dashed link moved off the developer's head.
5. The card sat on top of its base with a notch under the rounded corners: it now stands sunk in a slot, the base
   in two halves (back before the card, front lip after) with one seamless right face.

6. Fix pass on the critic and blind reader (it read as a tray, a server rack or a circuit board; the reviewer did
   nothing; the link was faint): the keyboard is now a low wedge at about 75% of the old length with real rows,
   keycaps with a cap top and a skirt, a long space bar and a tall lime Enter, so the pawn's head clears it; the
   card moved forward and left to fill the plate's upper left and balance on its centre; the faint floor line became
   one 2-wide dashed line straight back from Enter into the card's base; the reviewer stands at the card's
   front-left corner with a short shoulder arm on the sign-off button (raised within reach); the developer's arm
   now grows from the shoulder and the hand sits in the middle of Enter's lime top; the check rows are ruled apart
   and the code row carries two lines of output.

7. Final polish (pair with climatetech, and weight): the card standing upright in a base with a person at it
   repeated climatetech's monitor on a plinth with a designer at it. The result now lies flat on a white desk
   behind the keyboard, and the reviewer stands across the desk with a hand on its sign-off, so nothing in the
   picture is upright. The keyboard was a dark slab at 360: its margin went from 10 to 7, its height from 8–15 to
   5–11, the keycap outlines from brandeis-700 to brandeis-500 and the skirts from brandeis-200 to brandeis-100, so
   it reads as white keys on a thin blue tray. The dashed link is gone: the sheet lies right behind the keyboard on
   the same desk, and a 20-unit line read as noise. With nothing tall left, the trimmed box is 1.88:1; the floor
   and its width are healthtech's, so at 360 the plate renders at exactly healthtech's scale, only shorter.
