# /expertise/ai-products-developer-tools · hero

Slot: hero beside the copy, paper ground, renders about 600px wide and as the /expertise card cover at 360.
`hero.svg`, viewBox 864.3 × 576.2 (1.50:1), built by `build.py` (`python3 build.py` rewrites `hero.svg`).
Floor and projection are healthtech's, verbatim: `x = -144 + 0.9(u - v)`, `y = 27 + 0.45(u + v) - h`.

## The object

The hub has no object for this industry. Picked: **the developer's keyboard**, drawn the way the hub draws its
objects (a simple isometric volume, solid blue as the hero, white keys with brandeis-200 sides). It says
"developer tools" at a glance with no screen and no lettering; the AI half comes from the small "AI" badge on
the result.

## Candidate ideas

1. **Press Enter, get a result you can check.** A blue keyboard lies on the floor, its one lime Enter key pressed
   by a developer; behind it the first result stands in a low base, marked AI, with a line of code and the three
   checks the model ran, a reviewer beside it.
2. **The rubber duck.** A developer explains code to a rubber duck on a desk while an AI reply comes back. Clever,
   but a metaphor that needs the in-joke; fails the three-second read.
3. **The AI chip under review.** A large chip marked AI on a tray with a reviewer's tick stamp beside it. Stock, and
   it says "hardware", not product design.

**Pick: 1.** It is the page's own lead in one picture: "gets developers to a working first result" (the pressed
Enter) "and lets reviewers see what the model checked before they sign off" (the ticked card and its waiting
sign-off button, the reviewer beside it). It keeps the round-3 pick's "first call, clean response" idea, which
Jatin called "right, in a way", but trades the abstract blue code panel and the lime rod for a real object.

**Arrangement, checked against the siblings:** a long, low object lying across the front of the plate, with a
separate white card standing in its own base at the back. No phone, tablet or screen at all; nothing rises out of
the object (healthtech), nothing stands on a flat tablet (commerce, data), no stairs (edtech), no tile (proptech),
no folder (B2B), no wired monitor (climatetech).

## The drawing

- **Idea:** "They get a developer from pressing Enter to a working first result, and show the reviewer what the
  model checked before they sign off."
- **Drawn:** the family's dotted floor, dashed edge and soft inset. Across the front lies the keyboard at the hub's
  scale (a standing pawn clears it by more than a head): a low solid-blue wedge (blue top, brandeis-600 front,
  brandeis-700 end, 8 high at the front rising to 15 at the back) carrying five real rows of keycaps, each a white
  cap top on a brandeis-200 skirt: number row with a wide Backspace, Tab, wide Caps, two Shifts, and a long space
  bar at the front. At the right end of rows two and three is the one lime key, a tall Enter, pressed slightly down.
  A white client pawn marked with code brackets stands just past the keyboard's right end, its short light arm
  (blue-outlined) growing from the shoulder just under the head, the round hand centred on Enter's lime cap top.
  One dashed brandeis-500 line (2 wide, 6/5) runs straight back along the iso axis from under Enter into the front
  lip of a low white base. In the base's slot stands the result: a white card with a brandeis-200 edge, an "AI"
  badge and title bars, a pale code row (`< >` and two lines of output), three ticked rows ruled apart (what the
  model checked) and an outlined sign-off button with a tick. The reviewer, a white pawn marked with a tick, stands
  at the card's front-left corner with a short arm from the shoulder, the hand resting on the sign-off.
- **Composition:** the card stands back and left of centre, the keyboard in front of it, so the pair sits on the
  plate's centre with the press at the front right and the sign-off at the back left; the line ties them.
- **People:** both are the client's (a developer and a reviewer), so both are white with a blue outline and the same
  arm weight. No Tcules figure, chosen deliberately: the idea is who the product serves (the developer who wants
  a working result, the reviewer who signs it off), not how Tcules works with the client, as on healthtech.
- **Accent:** one lime, the Enter key. The sign-off button is outlined, not lime: it is the reviewer's to press.
- **Alt:** Isometric illustration for AI product and developer tool design. On a dotted floor lies a low blue
  keyboard with white keys, a long space bar and one tall lime Enter key, pressed by a developer marked with code
  brackets. A dashed line runs straight back from the keyboard into the low base of a white card standing behind
  it: the first result, marked AI, with a line of code and three ticked checks the model ran. A reviewer marked
  with a tick stands at the card's corner with a hand on its sign-off button.

## Rounds

1. Keyboard front-centre, card mid-plate: the developer stood in front of the keyboard covering its corner, the
   reviewer's arm was a long stick up to the card, and the box was 1.9:1 (too wide).
2. People moved (reviewer to the card's left with no arm, developer to the keyboard's right), viewBox trimmed;
   the developer's arm became a long pointer across the keys.
3. Enter moved to the front right, the developer computed to stand just right of it, so the arm is short; card
   moved to the back corner to raise the box.
4. Card shifted right and made taller (box 1.49:1, inside 5:4–3:2); an outlined sign-off button added at its foot;
   the dashed link moved off the developer's head.
5. The card sat on top of its base with a notch under the rounded corners: it now stands sunk in a slot, the base
   in two halves (back before the card, front lip after) with one seamless right face.

6. Fix pass on the critic and blind reader (it read as a tray, a server rack or a circuit board; the reviewer did
   nothing; the link was faint): the keyboard is now a low wedge at about 75% of the old length with real rows,
   keycaps with a cap top and a skirt, a long space bar and a tall lime Enter, so the pawn's head clears it; the
   card moved forward and left to fill the plate's upper left and balance on its centre; the faint floor line became
   one 2-wide dashed line straight back from Enter into the card's base; the reviewer stands at the card's
   front-left corner with a short shoulder arm on the sign-off button (raised within reach); the developer's arm
   now grows from the shoulder and the hand sits in the middle of Enter's lime top; the check rows are ruled apart
   and the code row carries two lines of output.
