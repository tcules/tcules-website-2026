# /why-tcules — "Better product decisions, before you build"

Three principle figures, one set: the same 458 × 402 box (`viewBox="0 0 458 402"`), the same floor diamond
(dashed brandeis-500 edge, brandeis-50 inset, thinned brandeis-300 dots), the same object scale, one hero
object each and a different centrepiece each. Drawn for a tint band (paper-deep `#f3f1ea`), rendered at about
400px wide.

## Candidate ideas

**1 · Evidence before screens** — "We test your assumptions with real users before they become expensive mistakes."
- A. A real user tries a small prototype and answers with a tick, while the full screen behind it is still only an outline. **(picked)**
- B. A magnifying glass held over a row of user pawns.
- C. A clipboard of ticked notes beside a screen that is still dashed.

**2 · What to build first** — "You leave knowing what to build, what can wait, and why."
- A. A Tcules person points at the lime block in the first slot of a tray; the second slot holds only a pale dashed block, the third only a dashed outline. **(picked)**
- B. A roadmap board with three cards, the top card lime.
- C. A Tcules person sorting boxes into "now" and "later" piles.

**3 · Design that pays back** — "Every design decision is tied to the adoption, renewals or revenue it should move."
- A. A dashed line runs from a design's lime button to the tallest of three rising bars. **(picked)**
- B. A stack of coins growing beside a screen.
- C. A button wired to a dial that turns up.

**Why these won.** Each one can be named in three seconds without the copy: someone trying a phone before the
real screen exists; one block picked first out of a row; a button wired to a rising chart. Each has a
different centrepiece: an upright phone, a flat tray of blocks, a column chart. Side by side they read as
siblings, not one picture repeated. The B and C options were either metaphors that need explaining
(magnifier, dial) or too close to each other (two of them were boards of cards).

## The drawings

### evidence-before-screens.svg
- **Idea:** "They test it with a real user, and get an answer, before the full product is built."
- **Drawn:** A large pale dashed screen stands at the back: the full product, not built yet (one header bar and
  one block, so it survives at 400px). In front, the hero: a small solid blue prototype phone, about 1.3 times
  the user's height, with a picture block, a heading bar and a lime button. A white pawn (the client's real
  user) stands close and taps the lime button with a short arm. Above the user, a white speech bubble with a
  blue tick: the user's answer, the evidence.
- **Alt:** "Isometric drawing: on a dotted floor, a real user taps the lime button on a small blue prototype
  phone and answers with a tick in a speech bubble, while the full screen behind them is still only a pale
  dashed outline, not yet built."

### what-to-build-first.svg
- **Idea:** "They tell you which piece to build first, and which can wait."
- **Drawn:** The hero is a solid blue tray with three slots in a row. Slot one holds a lime block, seated. Slot
  two holds only a pale dashed block (next). Slot three is only a dashed outline on the tray (later). A solid
  blue Tcules pawn stands at the front-left end of the tray and points at the lime block with a short arm.
- **Alt:** "Isometric drawing: on a dotted floor, a solid blue tray has three slots in a row. The first holds a
  lime block, the second only a pale dashed block, and the third is just a dashed outline. A Tcules person
  stands at the front and points to the lime block: build this first."

### design-that-pays-back.svg
- **Idea:** "Every design choice is tied to the number it should move."
- **Drawn:** A white design screen lies on the floor at the front left, on the inset: a lime button, a picture
  block, a heading bar and a text bar. Three bars rise from front to back: two white, then the tallest, the solid
  blue hero. A dashed line runs from the lime button's end, off the card, to the top of the blue bar, where a
  white-ringed node marks its height.
- **Alt:** "Isometric drawing: on a dotted floor, a white design screen with a lime button lies at the front. A
  dashed line runs from that button to the top of the tallest of three rising bars, which is solid blue."
