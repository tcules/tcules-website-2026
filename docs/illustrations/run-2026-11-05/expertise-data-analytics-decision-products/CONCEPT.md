## The object

The hub has no object for data and analytics, so this hero adds one that everyone reads the same way: **a bar
chart**. It is drawn as the hub draws its objects: white volumes with a blue outline and brandeis-200 side faces,
rising out of a product that lies flat (the hub's own pop-up-book move).

## Candidate ideas

1. **What changed, and why.** A bar chart stands on the floor; one bar has grown, its new part in lime, and a row on
   a laptop beside it explains it, its trend tile in the same lime. Two people from different teams read the same
   figure, one at the bar, one at the screen.
2. **A score with its reasons.** A gauge whose lime needle is fed by three short bars, each tied to it by a
   dashed line, so the score shows what it is based on.
3. **The same figure, two teams.** Two printed reports with different charts lie side by side, both carrying one
   identical lime figure tile (round 3's pick A).

## The pick: idea 1

The lead says "shows what changed and why, in figures every team reads alike". Idea 1 draws both halves of that sentence:
the lime growth is what changed, the explaining row is the reason, and two people with their hands on the bar and
on its row are teams reading the figure the same way. Idea 2 covers only one item from the "designs" list (Doodle's scores).
Idea 3 needs two charts, and the family allows one screen at most. A bar chart with one highlighted bar is
also the clearest way to show change at 360px.

Buyer's sentence: "They build analytics into the product that show customers what changed and why."
