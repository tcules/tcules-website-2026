## Candidate ideas

1. **The work on screen, the sketch beside it.** The desk as it really is: a light monitor with a page being designed
   on its blue screen and the one lime button selected, and in front of it an open sketchbook with the same page drawn
   by hand. The mug, books, plant and pulled-out chair make it someone's desk.
2. **The pinboard desk.** The same desk against a wall of pinned cards and swatches, so the process shows behind the
   screen.
3. **Two screens.** A monitor and a propped tablet or phone showing the same design at two sizes.

**Pick: 1.** It is the closest to the original subject and the calmest. The idea still reads without words: the page on
paper becomes the page on screen. Idea 2 adds a wall that the family's floor never has, and a pinboard turns into
noise at 360. Idea 3 turns the desk into a device diagram, which the service heroes already cover.

## What changed from the old desk

- **The new hand.** The grey-ink line and beige hatching are replaced by a blue line on white, flat brandeis tints on
  the side faces, rounded corners and the dotted floor diamond. There is no black anywhere.
- **The device rule (STYLE §3).** The monitor's body is light and only its screen is blue. The old monitor had a black
  bezel.
- **One accent (STYLE §4).** The lime moved from the sticky note on the book to the button being designed on screen,
  so the accent marks the work. The book carries a blue ribbon instead.
- **The sketch echoes the screen.** The old sketchbook held a generic dotted box. It now shows the screen's page.
- **The plant** is new. It fills the empty back-right corner and makes the desk someone's own. It is kept to three
  plain leaves.
