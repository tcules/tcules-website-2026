Floor and projection are healthtech's verbatim (STYLE §9a): `x = -144 + 0.9(u - v)`, `y = 27 + 0.45(u + v) - h`.

The hub has no marketplace object, so this page brings one: **a balance scale**, the real object for two sides
held level by one thing between them.

## Why the desk calendar was dropped (fix pass, 5 Nov)

The blind reader named a scheduling tool (Calendly) and reached "marketplace" only when pushed. A desk calendar
is one person's tool, not the place two sides meet; its two pawns were mirror images showing the same lime tick,
so the asymmetry between the sides, which is the page's argument, was lost. It was also oversized (about 70% of
the frame) and pushed both people to the plate's corners. Verdict: redraw.

## Candidate ideas (from the critic's list and one more)

1. **A booking ticket with a perforated stub**, one half held by each side. Reads as events or travel tickets
   before marketplaces, and a flat ticket lying on the floor repeats commerce and data's flat-object formula.
2. **A two-sided service hatch** with a different app facing each side. Reads as a shop counter: the "typical
   marketplace" picture Bhumika called cute.
3. **One web platform as a light laptop** with a phone on each side. The laptop is commerce's product now.
4. **A balance scale with each side's app on its own pan, the beam held level at one booking.** Two sides, two
   different apps, one platform holding them in balance: the "multi-sided" shape is the object itself.

**Pick: 4.** One sentence from the lead: "They design the platform that keeps both sides of a marketplace in
balance: buyers see providers they can trust, providers see requests worth taking, and one booking holds them
level." It answers the page's lines ("buyers book providers they trust, and providers spot requests worth
taking", "separate apps for each side, joined by one web platform", "the teams that run them") and carries
liquidity and trust without a word. The arrangement is new in the family: a tall, open, symmetrical object at
the centre with its two products hanging at the sides. No phone rises from it (healthtech), nothing lies flat
under it (commerce, data), nothing is wired to a screen (climatetech), no stairs, tile, folder, easel, cabinet,
keyboard or terminal.

## The drawing

- **Idea:** "They design the platform that keeps both sides in balance, each with its own app, held level by one
  booking."
- **Drawn:** the family's dotted floor with a dashed edge and the brandeis-50 inset. Centre-back stands the scale
  (the hero object): a white iso base with a brandeis-200 side, a solid blue column with a brandeis-600 half for
  its roundness, and a level blue beam with a brandeis-700 thickness strip. Each end has a ring; from it three
  thin chains hang to a shallow white pan with a brandeis-200 rim. On the left pan stands the **buyer's app**, a
  blue phone facing front-left: a provider's portrait card, a name bar, a row of four stars and a book button.
  On the right pan stands the **provider's app**, a blue phone facing front-right: a request's title and detail
  bars, a pin (where), a clock (when) and a large accept button with a tick. The two screens are deliberately
  different: the asymmetry between the sides.
- **Accent:** one lime thing, the booking, as a disc with a tick at the pivot where the beam meets the column:
  the one thing both sides hang from. Neither phone carries lime.
- **People (§5):** the buyer (white client pawn) at the left pan's outer side and the provider (white client
  pawn) at the right pan's outer side, each with a short light arm in a blue outline from the shoulder, a hand
  on the rim of their own pan. A Tcules designer (solid blue, white edge, the arm drawn over the body so it
  leaves the shoulder as one shape) stands at the scale's foot with a hand on the base: the people who design
  the platform. All three stand on the inset, near what they use. No badges: roles are shown by what each
  side's phone displays.
- **Scale:** the scale spans about 54% of the frame's width, an open frame with its mass in the column, beam and
  two phones; the phones are 70 × 120, a little smaller than healthtech's phone, and the pawns are healthtech's
  size exactly (R 22, H 62, head 16, arm 11 including the outline, hand r 8.8).
- **Nothing floats:** the base stands on the floor; the pans hang by visible chains from the beam; the phones
  stand on the pans.
- **Screens:** two phones; §9a's exception, two people's views of the same booking.
- **Alt:** Isometric illustration for marketplace product design. A balance scale stands on a dotted floor, its
  blue beam held level at one lime booking with a tick. On the left pan stands the buyer's app, showing a
  provider with a portrait, a row of stars and a book button; on the right pan stands the provider's app,
  showing a request with a place, a time and an accept button. A buyer stands at the left pan and a provider at
  the right, each reaching to their own app, and a blue Tcules designer stands at the scale's foot.
