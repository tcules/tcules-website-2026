# /services hero: concept

Slot: the hero of `/services`, centred under the title, on paper. Wide (the drawing is 1.80:1), rendered up to 860px.

Copy, as authored:
- Title: "UI/UX design services for new and established SaaS, through to working software"
- Lead: "UI/UX design leads every project. When we also build, our designers stay with it until release, and nothing gets lost between design and code."

## Candidate ideas

1. **"Their designer puts the design into the working product themselves."** (Round 1 pick, now dropped.) A
   designer sets a selected card into its slot on a running laptop. The critic and a blind reader showed it
   repeats design-engineering (handled card, avatar, pill, a pawn moving it) and product-design (dashed
   lines back into a blue screen), and reads as a stock "UI design" isometric.
2. **"Design goes first, and the build follows it to release."** A designer at the front of a short train.
   A metaphor that needs explaining; looks like a toy beside the approved heroes.
3. **"One continuous road from sketch to release."** Round A improved. Its centrepiece, a long track of
   stages ending in a product, is already the new-product-design-first-release hero.
4. **"Their designer is still there when real people start using it, and it still matches the sketch."**
   The lead's distinctive claim, the designers stay until release, drawn as a moment rather than a
   process: working software in use, the designer beside it, the designer's sketch on the floor.

## The pick, and why

**Idea 4.** It draws the claim that sets the page apart ("our designers stay with it until release") rather
than the category ("we design UI"). The laptop is in use by the client's people, so it is working software,
not a mock. The Tcules designer is there with them, a fingertip on the live button. "Nothing gets lost"
needs no connector: the sketch on the floor has the screen's layout block for block, and its one emphasised
block (the button, dashed in blue) is the one lime thing on the screen. The eye runs sketch, button,
designer, users. No other hero uses a laptop, a sketch on the floor, or a designer among users.

## The drawing

- **File:** `hero.svg`, generated by `hero.gen.mjs` (world coordinates; run
  `node docs/illustrations/run-2026-11-05/services/hero.gen.mjs <out.svg>`). The screen and the sketch read
  one shared layout table, so they cannot drift apart.
- **Idea:** their designer is still there when real people start using it, and it still matches the sketch.
- **What is drawn:** the family floor (dashed edge, 22-pitch dots, 50 inset), the laptop centred on it. The
  laptop is the hero object, solid blue: the lid has 12 rounded top corners and 600/700 thickness, the deck
  has rx 10 and 600/700 faces, a recessed keyboard plate with three long key bars, and a trackpad. The
  screen runs a product: a sidebar, a content panel, and a card with an avatar and the lime button (the
  one accent). In front, two white client pawns: one has a hand on the trackpad, the other stands
  beside. On the right, the solid Tcules pawn, tilted toward the screen, touches the lime button with a
  short arm. In front of the designer, flat on the floor, is a white sheet (outlined on every face) with the
  same layout in brandeis-300 dashes, the button dashed in brandeis-500.
- **Alt text:** An open laptop, solid blue, running a product: a sidebar, a content panel and a card with a
  lime button. Two of the client's people stand in front of it, one with a hand on the trackpad, using it.
  A Tcules designer stands beside the screen with a fingertip on the lime button. At the designer's feet
  lies the designer's sketch: a white sheet with the same layout drawn in dashes, the button marked, block
  for block like the screen.
