# /expertise/edtech-learning · hero

Slot: the hero beside the copy, on paper, rendered about 600px wide, and the card cover on /expertise at about 360px.
`hero.svg`, viewBox 801.8 × 529 (about 1.52:1), written by `build.mjs` beside it (`node build.mjs`).

## Candidate ideas (all built on the hub's books and graduation cap)

1. **A staircase of books.** The hub's books rise as a staircase toward the graduation cap, a learner climbs it and
   reaches for the next step, and a phone beside it shows that same next step as one lime button.
2. **The cap on a phone.** The hub's books and cap sit on a phone lying flat, with one lime lesson card rising out of
   the screen toward the learner. (This was the stopped first draft.)
3. **The teacher's shelf.** On a teacher's laptop each learner is a pile of books growing toward a cap, and the one
   short pile that has stalled is flagged, so the teacher sees who is stuck.

## The pick: idea 1

It is the most literal reading of the page's lead ("learners get a clear next step, even on a phone"), and it answers
Jatin's note on round 3 ("you can't see that a student is learning"): the learner stands a step below the next book and
reaches up for it, so the climb is still under way. The hub's object is kept whole and made into the path, so a visitor
coming from /expertise knows the field at once. The phone is tied to the climb: a dashed line runs from its lime
next-step button straight to the lime book, so the button in the app IS the learner's next step up. Idea 2 (the first
draft) read as a big empty flat phone, and its books looked like slabs. Idea 3 is the second half of the lead, so it
would make two ideas in one picture. Its teacher view is left to the page's copy.

- **Idea, as a buyer would say it:** "They design learning apps whose next-step button is the learner's next step up."
- **Hero object:** the solid blue phone (white screen, a dark blue side), standing to the right of the stairs, its
  face in line with the third pile. It is about 80% of its earlier height, so the cap sits near its mid-height and
  the stairs, not the phone, carry the story.
- **Accent:** lime twice, as the two halves of one idea joined by the dashed line: the next book (the step the learner
  reaches for) and the phone's next-step button.

## Parts

- The family's floor: a dashed brandeis-500 edge, brandeis-300 dots and a brandeis-50 inset, trimmed tight to the
  drawing.
- Four piles of books (one, two, three and four books), climbing toward the back right. Each book is built as in
  `expertise.svg`: light covers (brandeis-100 top, 200 and 300 sides), a white page block with two page lines on the
  front face. The piles are slightly staggered so they read as books, not slabs.
- The next step: the top book of the third pile, with lime covers.
- The hub's graduation cap on the fourth (top) pile, on plain books: a dark blue crown, a solid blue board with a
  white button and a white tassel.
- A learner (a client's user): a white pawn with a blue outline on the second step. The arm is light with a blue
  outline, the family's one arm weight, and its hand reaches the front edge of the lime book's cover.
- A solid blue phone standing upright, showing a lesson: a title bar, a video panel with a play button and a progress
  bar, a course progress row of four steps (two done, as on the stairs), one text line and a lime next-step button
  with a chevron.
- The projection: a dashed brandeis-500 line (4/4) from the button's left end, along the iso −x axis in the plane of
  the phone's screen, to the lime book's cover, ending in a small dot on its edge.
- No Tcules figure. The idea is about what the learner gets, not about who does the work (STYLE §5: people only where
  the idea is about who does what).

## Alt

Isometric illustration for EdTech product design. Piles of books rise as a staircase, one book higher each step, toward a graduation cap on the top pile. A learner stands on the second step and reaches up for the next one, a lime book. Beside the stairs stands a blue phone showing a lesson: a video, a course progress bar with two of four steps done, and a lime next-step button. A dashed line runs from that button to the lime book, so the next step in the app is the next step up for the learner.

## Rounds

- v0 (the stopped draft, `preview/v0-staircase-on-flat-phone.png`): book steps on a phone lying flat. The phone was a
  large empty plate, the books read as slabs and the cap's crown was hidden.
- v1: an upright phone and three book piles climbing along +x. In iso the height gain cancels out against the
  piles moving down the screen, so the stairs looked flat, and the two halves were far apart.
- v2: the stairs turned to climb along −y (up and to the right on screen), so the climb reads at once.
- v3: four steps, thicker books, and the next book's cover in lime. The phone was moved forward to stand beside the
  top pile, so the drawing is one group, and it is now drawn after the piles because it is nearer the viewer. The
  floor was trimmed.
- v4: a smaller cap set back so the lime cover shows, a larger learner, a tighter stair pitch and floor, joins checked
  at 2000px (the arm meets the body, the cap's crown sits under the board, and the phone overlaps the top pile in the
  right order).
- v5 (fix pass after the blind read and critic; before: `preview/v4-before-fix.png`): the learner moved down to the
  second step, reaching up for the lime book on the third, and the cap now sits on plain books on the fourth, so the
  learner is visibly mid-climb rather than arriving. The phone is about 80% of its height, stands clear to the right
  of the stairs with its face in line with the third pile, and a dashed line runs along −x from its lime button to
  the lime book's cover. The quiz block on the screen became a four-step progress row (two done). The floor and the
  stair pitch were tightened so the stairs and the learner hold up at 360. Joins checked at 2000px: the hand on the
  cover's edge, the line's dot on the cover corner, the tassel clear of the phone, the line clear of the top pile.
